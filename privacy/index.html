Privacy

Last updated 12 November 2026

In short. Installed Fluxkit tools run on your own machines. If you choose a hosted feature, such as ubercal or Cora's Google email connection, we process only the data needed for that feature, with the Google access you grant. We protect credentials with encryption, never sell Google data or use it for ads or model training, and explain below how to disconnect and delete it.

01Who we are

Fluxkit is made by meaningtech. meaningtech decides how the data described here is used (the data controller). You can reach us at hello@meaningtech.io for anything on this page.

02What this covers

This policy covers fluxkit.dev, ubercal, and the hosted Google authorization service at connect.fluxkit.dev used by Cora's optional Google email connection. Google shows the name Fluxkit on the consent screen for these features.

Installing an open-source tool in the kit does not by itself send us its data. Its operator controls its local storage and any other providers it connects. If you enable Cora's optional Google email connection, the authorization service described here participates in that connection; Cora itself continues to run on the operator's infrastructure.

03Signing in with Google

When you sign in to ubercal, Google tells us your Google account ID, your email address, your name and your profile picture (openid, email, profile). We use them to identify you and show who is signed in. A signed, strictly necessary session cookie, fluxkit_session, lasts up to 30 days and holds your sign-in identity.

Cora's Google connection requests openid and email to confirm the connected account's identity and verified sender address. It does not request your profile picture or Calendar access. Calendar permissions and Gmail permissions are requested separately, when you choose the corresponding feature. The additional Gmail permissions described below are shown by Google when you connect full mailbox access.

04ubercal

ubercal merges calendars into share links. To do that it keeps:

  • Google Calendar data, only if you connect Google Calendar, through Google's read-only calendar permission (calendar.readonly): the list of your calendars, and the events of the calendars you choose to add, with their times, titles, descriptions, places, attendees, conference links and repeat rules. This read-only permission never creates, changes or deletes calendar data.
  • Google access tokens for each Google account you connect, encrypted at rest, so ubercal can read those calendars again every five minutes, and when a calendar app asks for a share link.
  • Calendar links you paste (.ics or webcal), encrypted at rest because they often contain a private key, and the events they contain.
  • Your share links: their names, their secret addresses, how each one shows each calendar, which dates it covers, and when a calendar app last read it.
  • Your booking pages, only if you make one: their names, durations, weekly hours, time zone and limits. To take bookings, ubercal asks Google for leave to add events to calendars you own (calendar.events.owned). It uses that leave only to write each booked meeting into the calendar you choose, so Google sends the invitation; it never changes or deletes your other events.
  • People who book you: the name, email address and notes they enter on your booking page, and the time they chose. They reach you as a Google Calendar invitation and an email notice, the person who booked gets a confirmation and reminders by email, and ubercal keeps these details so your page can show what is coming up, until 30 days after the meeting.

We use this data for two purposes: to build and refresh the share links you make, and to offer and record the bookings your booking pages take. A share link shows to whoever has its address exactly what you chose: a calendar set to full with its details, a calendar set to busy with its times only and nothing else, a calendar set to off not at all. Treat a share link like a password; you can replace it with a new address at any time and the old one stops working at once.

05Connected Gmail: Cora and Milo

You can connect a Google address in Cora's Settings → Connectors, then choose which of your agents may use it. A send-only connection requests gmail.send, alongside the account identity permissions above, and cannot read existing messages. When full mailbox access is enabled, Cora also requests gmail.modify and gmail.compose. These allow the selected agents to read and search message headers, bodies and attachments; manage read/unread status, stars and labels; archive messages or move them to and from the trash; create, read and delete drafts; and send the messages you authorize. The tools available to an agent are limited by the permissions Google actually granted.

  • Authorization. The hosted service exchanges and refreshes Google credentials over HTTPS, verifies your email address, and binds the authorization to the Cora instance that requested it. Google's return passes through ubercal's registered callback to this service and then back to your instance.
  • Credentials. Your Cora instance stores the refresh credential and its signed authorization grant encrypted in hush. Its local connection record keeps your email address, granted permission, connection date, connecting user and chosen agents. Short-lived access tokens are held in memory.
  • Messages and drafts. Your Cora instance makes the requested searches, reads and mailbox changes directly through the Gmail API. When you send, Google receives the recipients, subject, body and any included content, and delivers the message. Gmail stores drafts created through the connection. The hosted authorization service exchanges credentials only: it does not receive message bodies, attachments or recipients, and does not read or keep an inbox.
  • Your instance and model provider. Message content and metadata read for a task can enter the agent's conversation and be processed by its configured model provider. Your instance may retain those conversations, tool results, sent-mail activity, downloaded attachments and artifacts under its operator's storage and access rules. Google credentials are not given to the model. Google data is not used to train models; the instance operator chooses the provider and account settings and must keep this processing within Google's Limited Use requirements.

The Google connection belongs to the user who linked it. An agent can use it only after that user grants it access. Removing an agent's grant prevents further reads and other Gmail operations through that connection.

Milo. Milo is an installed email client using a separate OAuth client under the same Fluxkit Google project. It requests gmail.modify to read and synchronize the connected mailbox, organize messages and send mail. Its operator-controlled service stores the mail archive, account settings and drafts, and its clients may keep device caches for offline use. If the operator enables its optional AI features, selected email content is processed by the configured model provider. This data does not pass through Cora's hosted authorization service; the operator manages the service, device caches, model settings and deletion.

06Google user data: Limited Use

Fluxkit's use and transfer to any other app of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In particular:

  • we use Google user data only to provide the visible calendar, booking and connected-email features you choose;
  • we transfer it only as needed for those features with your consent: to Google, to the people who receive your calendar links, bookings or emails, and to the service providers described here; or for security or legal requirements;
  • we do not use it for advertising, we do not sell it, and we do not use it to train AI or machine learning models;
  • no person at meaningtech reads it, unless you ask us to for support, it is needed for security or to investigate abuse, or the law requires it.

07How long we keep it

  • Events are replaced every time ubercal reads their calendar; an event deleted at the source disappears from ubercal at the next read.
  • Removing a calendar deletes its events. Disconnecting a Google account deletes its calendars and asks Google to revoke ubercal's access.
  • Deleting your account, from the button at the bottom of the app, deletes your calendars, events, share links, booking pages, bookings, connected accounts and profile at once and revokes ubercal's Google access.
  • The details of someone who booked you are deleted 30 days after the meeting. If you booked someone and want your details removed sooner, write to us.
  • You can also remove ubercal's access from your Google account at any time, at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • In Cora, disconnecting a Google connection disables it, removes all agent grants and clears cached credentials from memory. Its encrypted hush entry and local history can remain on the instance until the operator removes them. Disconnecting does not delete Gmail messages or drafts, or conversations, downloaded attachments and artifacts already kept by the instance.
  • To revoke Google's authorization as well, remove Fluxkit at myaccount.google.com/connections. Because the Google app is shared, revocation can also disconnect other Fluxkit Google features you use. Ask your Cora instance operator to delete its local connection history and stored credentials; contact us for data held by the hosted service.

08How we protect Google data

  • Encrypted connections. Public access, Google authorization, token exchange and API requests use HTTPS to protect data in transit.
  • Encrypted credentials. ubercal encrypts stored Google access and refresh tokens and private calendar URLs with AES-256-GCM. Cora keeps its Google refresh credential and authorization grant encrypted in hush on the instance. Encryption keys and application secrets are kept separate from public source code.
  • Access controls. Signed sessions identify the user. ubercal checks account ownership before returning private calendars or settings. Cora checks the connecting user's ownership and the agent grants before permitting Gmail operations. Secret credentials are excluded from the agent's model environment.
  • Protected authorization. Temporary state and PKCE checks bind a Google authorization to the request that started it. Cora's hosted service validates the registered instance and signed grant before exchanging or refreshing its credential.
  • Limited exposure. We request permissions for the feature in use: read-only calendars for merging, owned-calendar event writes for bookings, and the Gmail permissions needed for the connected email features described above. Send-only Cora connections do not gain mailbox-reading access. Share-link addresses are random and can be replaced. Calendar event contents and Google tokens are excluded from application logs.

Cora’s instance operator manages the security of its machine, local archives, backups and chosen providers.

09Where it is and who helps

The hosted services run on servers meaningtech operates; ubercal's database is on the same machine. Public traffic uses HTTPS through a relay we run on DigitalOcean. Google provides sign-in, the Calendar data you choose to share, the connected Gmail mailbox and draft operations, and delivery of email sent through Gmail. Resend sends ubercal's booking emails (confirmation, reminders and the owner's notice), receiving the booking name, time and notes, the guest's name and email, and the owner's email. Cloudflare answers DNS queries for fluxkit.dev. Cora's instance, its storage and any model providers are chosen by the instance operator, as explained above.

10Cookies and analytics

ubercal uses only the sign-in cookies described above: fluxkit_session, and a short-lived cookie that protects the sign-in from forgery for ten minutes while you are at Google. It has no analytics and no trackers. The fluxkit.dev home page uses Google Analytics to count visits; the legal pages and the hosted services do not.

11Your rights

Wherever you live, you can ask us to show you the data we hold about you, correct it, delete it, or give it to you in a portable format, and you can object to how we use it. Most of this you can do yourself in the app. For the rest, write to hello@meaningtech.io; we answer within 30 days. If you are in the European Union or the United Kingdom, you can also complain to your data protection authority. We use your data to provide the service you asked for (contract) and, for the security of the service, on our legitimate interest.
